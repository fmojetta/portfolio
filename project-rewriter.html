Rewriter

Side project · macOS menu-bar app · August 2026

The itch

Most of what I write during the day goes into Slack, email, docs and Figma comments, often in my second language. A quick “make this clearer” or “make this more formal” pass right where I’m typing was the Grammarly feature I actually used. Copying text into a chat window and back breaks the flow, so I wanted the rewrite to come to the text, not the other way round.

How it works

  • Select text anywhere and press ⌥⌘R.
  • A floating preview shows the rewrite in the chosen mode: improve clarity, fix grammar only, more formal, more concise, more friendly, translate Italian ↔ English, or a custom prompt.
  • Accept to paste it back in place, tweak the text first, switch mode, or regenerate.
  • The clipboard is saved and restored around every rewrite, so whatever you had copied is still there afterwards.
  • Every mode’s prompt can be edited in Settings, which is where most of the tuning happened.

Why a small local model

It talks to a local Ollama server (qwen2.5 7B by default), or to any OpenAI-compatible endpoint if I want something bigger. A 7B model won’t match the large hosted ones on style, but for rewording a Slack message it’s fast, private and free to run. Reasoning models get their hidden “thinking” switched off automatically, which turns a minute-long wait back into a couple of seconds.

What I learned

This was my hands-on way into local LLM tooling, and it paid off. The hard parts weren’t the model. They were the small details that make a tool feel trustworthy: not clobbering the clipboard, returning focus to the right app, explaining clearly why macOS needs Accessibility access, and treating a slow or empty model reply as an error instead of something that could get pasted. A small model with tight prompts goes a long way.

Where it stands

I use it every day. I shared a short demo with colleagues, and it got people curious about trying small local models themselves. That was half the point.
