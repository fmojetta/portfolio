Screenshot Shelf

Side project · macOS menu-bar app · September 2026

The itch

⌘⌃⇧4 is the fastest way to grab part of the screen: no file on the desktop, just paste it where it’s needed. The catch is that the clipboard only holds one thing. During competitor research or a design review I’d capture three or four screens in a row, then realise only the last one was still there. Saving to files instead means a cluttered desktop and a trip to Finder every time.

How it works

  • It watches the clipboard in the background. When something that looks like a screenshot lands there (image data only, no file, link or text with it), it saves a copy to the shelf.
  • Images copied from a browser or another app bring extra data along, so they’re left alone and the shelf stays screenshots only.
  • Click a thumbnail to copy it again. ⌘-click or shift-click to pick several, then drag them all at once into any app.
  • Arrow keys move around the grid and holding space opens a Quick Look preview, just like in Finder.
  • Right-click to show in Finder or delete, plus Clear, Open Folder and Launch at login in the footer.

Small on purpose

The whole app is a single Swift file of about 650 lines (SwiftUI for the panel, a bit of AppKit where SwiftUI falls short), built by a short shell script with no Xcode project. Dragging several screenshots at once is a good example: SwiftUI’s drag modifier carries only one item, so each thumbnail has a thin AppKit layer on top that starts the drag itself and stacks the files into a pile. Details like that, plus keeping the panel open while a drag is in flight or while the preview has focus, are what make it feel native rather than bolted on.

Where it stands

It launches at login and I use it every day. It has quietly replaced the screenshots folder on my desktop. A build script packages it into a drag-to-install disk image, so it’s easy to share with anyone who wants it.
